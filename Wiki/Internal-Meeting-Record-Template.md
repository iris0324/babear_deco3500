# Internal Meeting Records

This page documents key internal team discussions, decisions and coordination activities throughout the project.

Not all project coordination occurred in formal meetings. Some decisions were made asynchronously through the team group chat, shared documents, Miro and GitHub. The records below were reconstructed from contemporaneous team messages and project artefacts rather than created retrospectively as fictional meeting minutes.

---

## 20 August 2026 — Problem Space and Design Opportunity

### Participants
Team Babear members participating in the group discussion.

### Current Progress
The team was refining the project problem space and research direction for the art-gallery domain.

### Discussion & Decisions
The team discussed the initial problem as:

> Visitors often experience artworks individually and passively, with limited opportunities to understand how other visitors interpret or respond to the same artwork.

The research question was refined around how social and mobile technologies might encourage visitors to engage actively with artworks and exchange interpretations without disrupting the gallery experience.

The team also articulated an early design opportunity:

> How might we help visitors discover and exchange different interpretations of artworks through lightweight social interaction, without distracting attention from the physical artwork?

### Issues & Feedback
The team recognised that the project needed to balance social interaction with attention to physical artworks rather than simply adding more digital content.

### Next Steps
- continue literature and domain research;
- investigate visitor interpretation behaviour;
- explore concepts that support lightweight perspective exchange.

## 21 August 2026 — Refining the Art Persona Concept

### Participants
Team Babear members participating in the group discussion.

### Current Progress
The team was developing **Art Persona** as an early concept for supporting artwork interpretation.

### Discussion & Decisions
A concern was raised that describing Art Persona as an art "personality test" was not sufficiently supported by the research.

The available research supported visitors forming their own interpretations and learning how other people interpreted artworks, but did not demonstrate a need for permanent personality categorisation.

The concept was therefore reframed as a more dynamic interpretation-based experience rather than a fixed personality type.

### Issues & Feedback
The team identified a risk of designing a feature because it sounded engaging rather than because it was supported by research evidence.

### Next Steps
- frame Art Persona around visitors' responses to artworks;
- avoid claiming that the system measures a permanent personality;
- ensure presentation wording is traceable to research findings.

## 14 October 2026 — Round 3 User Testing

### Participants
Team Babear members involved in Iteration 3 testing and evaluation, together with recruited test participants.

### Current Progress
By this stage, the project had moved beyond the asynchronous visitor-trace structure of Iteration 2 and was testing more direct and collaborative forms of social interaction.

Two main interaction structures were evaluated:

- **Blind Eyes** — a two-person interaction using asymmetric information, questioning and artwork guessing;
- **Face-to-Face collaborative interaction** — a small-group activity based on clue sharing, discussion and collective decision-making.

### Testing Activities
The team conducted:

- four two-player Blind Eyes rounds;
- one three-person Face-to-Face session.

The purpose was to investigate whether the new interaction structures could create more active perspective exchange than simply showing another visitor's interpretation.

### Key Observations
Across the Blind Eyes rounds, the team recorded:

- perspective-based questions;
- interpretive explanations;
- substantive responses;
- differences in interpretation;
- newly noticed artwork details.

The testing showed that the interaction successfully generated communication around artworks.

However, an important issue also became visible:

> **More communication did not automatically mean deeper perspective exchange.**

Some participants used perspective-related questions mainly as strategies for identifying the correct artwork rather than as opportunities to understand another person's interpretation.

The Face-to-Face session also showed active clue sharing and negotiation, but a quieter participant found it harder to contribute verbally.

### Decisions
The team agreed that the next refinement should not simply maximise the amount of communication.

Instead, the design should focus on:

- whether participants respond to and build on another person's perspective;
- whether another person's contribution changes what someone notices;
- whether social structures allow quieter participants to contribute;
- whether interaction moves beyond task completion.

### Next Steps
- analyse Round 3 observations in more detail;
- combine testing findings with survey evidence;
- refine the interaction structure of Iteration 3;
- reconsider how collaboration and perspective exchange are represented in the interface.

## 15–18 October 2026 — Analysis, Survey Synthesis and Iteration 3 Refinement

### Participants
Team Babear members working across research analysis, UX, documentation and prototype refinement.

### Current Progress
Following Round 3 testing, the team consolidated user-testing observations with the exploratory survey conducted with **30 respondents**.

The survey indicated substantial variation in visitors' preferred levels of social participation:

- **21/30 (70%)** enjoyed viewing artworks quietly;
- only **9/30 (30%)** felt confident sharing interpretations with other visitors;
- respondents were interested in discovering new details and perspectives;
- concerns included communication difficulty, unresponsive partners, social pressure and dominant group members.

### Discussion & Analysis
The team compared these survey findings with the Round 3 testing results.

Two important design problems became clearer:

1. **One social mode could not suit every gallery visitor.**
2. **Direct communication alone did not guarantee meaningful perspective exchange.**

Iteration 2 had mainly supported asynchronous exposure to another visitor's perspective. The testing and survey suggested that Iteration 3 needed to support a wider spectrum of social involvement.

### Design Decisions
The team reframed the design around **flexible social participation**.

The emerging system included:

- independent / solo exploration;
- updated **Borrowed Eyes** team interaction;
- paired **Blind Eyes** interaction;
- small-group collaborative interaction;
- **Leave Your Eyes** for asynchronous contribution;
- later integration of **Live Gallery Chat**.

The team also retained an important principle from earlier iterations:

> Social interaction should be available without making participation compulsory.

### Next Steps
- translate the revised interaction logic into detailed user flows;
- refine how Play Alone and Play Together connect;
- create stronger roles for different participants;
- integrate research findings into the front-end structure;
- document Round 3 survey and testing findings in GitHub.

## 18–19 October 2026 — Interaction Flow and Front-End Redesign

### Participants
Team members contributing to interaction design, UX/UI, technical development and documentation.

### Current Progress
The team began translating Iteration 3 from research findings and conceptual activities into a more complete digital interaction system.

The design moved from isolated prototype activities toward an integrated mobile website / app-style experience.

### Interaction Flow Changes

#### Login and Location
A new entry flow was developed around:

- account creation and login;
- location permission;
- gallery verification;
- limited access when location is unavailable or the visitor is outside the gallery.

This allowed the system to distinguish between general browsing and interactions that depend on physical gallery presence.

#### Borrowed Eyes
Borrowed Eyes was expanded from a mainly asynchronous visitor-trace experience into a flexible interaction supporting:

- **Play Alone**;
- **Play Together**;
- finding or creating a team;
- team lobby and challenge setup;
- individual comments;
- clue unlocking;
- clue sharing through team chat;
- collective artwork guessing.

A key design change was that contribution now had a functional role:

> visitors contributed their own response in order to unlock information that could later support the team.

This was intended to prevent visitor-generated interpretations from becoming only a passive comment feed.

#### Blind Eyes
Blind Eyes was refined into a paired interaction with two different roles:

- **Eye / Describer**
- **Finder / Guesser**

The pair receives different information and must communicate through factual and perspective-based questions.

Progressive clues and repeated guesses were added to support continued interaction without immediately revealing the answer.

### UX / UI Refinement
The front-end was updated to support a more coherent visual and interaction system across:

- navigation;
- cards and artwork fragments;
- team interactions;
- chat;
- clues;
- maps;
- guessing;
- success/failure states;
- profile and contribution features.

### Issues & Feedback
The team recognised that the digital interface had become more complex than the earlier paper prototype.

The challenge was therefore to add social functionality without:

- overwhelming the visitor;
- increasing unnecessary screen time;
- losing the connection to physical artworks;
- making group interaction compulsory.

### Next Steps
- complete and review the detailed interaction flows;
- align web/app screens with the research-driven interaction logic;
- test consistency between individual, paired and group modes;
- continue implementation of the functional prototype.

### Prototype Development
The prototype work brought research, UX and technical implementation into the same system.

Development included:

- integrating artwork data and visual assets;
- implementing front-end interaction logic;
- refining navigation and page states;
- connecting the main Borrowed Eyes experience to collaborative functions;
- integrating the interaction structures developed through Iteration 3;
- reviewing visual consistency and usability;
- documenting setup and prototype structure for the repository.

### Discussion & Decisions
The team treated the final prototype as a **proof of concept**, rather than claiming that every production-level function had been fully implemented.

The focus was on demonstrating the core social/mobile experience:

> **different visitors can interact with artworks through different levels of social participation, while their observations and contributions influence what other visitors can do or notice.**

The team also continued to refine the distinction between:

- features that were functionally implemented;
- features represented through interface simulation;
- features that would require further technical development in a real gallery deployment.

### Issues
Key unresolved issues included:

- location accuracy and gallery verification;
- moderation of visitor-generated content;
- privacy and account data;
- participation inequality in groups;
- maintaining artwork attention while adding chat and social features;
- ensuring that communication supports perspective exchange rather than only task completion.

### Next Steps
- complete final visual and interaction refinements;
- document functional versus simulated prototype components;
- update Wiki and repository documentation;
- prepare poster and exhibition material;
- prepare the final prototype for demonstration.

## October Iteration Summary

October represented the most intensive development stage of the project.

During this period, the team completed a full iteration cycle:

> **Prototype refinement → user testing → evaluation → research synthesis → interaction redesign → front-end development → integrated digital prototype**

Iteration 3 therefore did not emerge from a single design decision. It developed through repeated movement between user evidence, social interaction analysis, UX design and technical implementation.

The central shift was:

> **Iteration 2: exposing visitors to another person's perspective**  
> ↓  
> **Iteration 3: designing different social structures in which visitors' contributions become interdependent**

This process led to the current system of flexible individual, paired and collaborative gallery interactions.
