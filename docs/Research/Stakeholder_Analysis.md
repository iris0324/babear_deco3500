# Stakeholder Analysis

## 1. Overview

The main stakeholder is the **gallery visitor**. Our project is designed around their experience of looking at and interpreting artworks.

Our primary focus is on **casual and less-experienced gallery visitors**. They may have limited art knowledge or feel unsure about forming their own interpretations.

Our stakeholder understanding changed during the design process.

In our first design iteration, **Art Persona**, the visitor was mainly an individual user. Visitors observed an artwork, answered short questions, received a Persona, and could view other visitors' responses.

However, the social interaction was limited. This led to the development of **Borrowed Eyes**.

In Borrowed Eyes, visitors interact with content left by previous visitors. They form their own interpretation, compare perspectives, and create something for a future visitor.

This changed the role of the visitor:

> **Art Persona: Visitor = User**  
> ↓  
> **Borrowed Eyes: Visitor = User + Contributor**

We therefore revised our stakeholder analysis to consider both the needs of visitors and their new role as contributors.

---

## 2. Stakeholder Identification

### 2.1 Primary Stakeholders — Casual and Less-Experienced Gallery Visitors

Our primary stakeholders are casual and less-experienced gallery visitors.

This may include visitors who:

- have limited formal knowledge of art;
- are unsure where to begin with an unfamiliar artwork;
- lack confidence in expressing a personal interpretation.

Previous research suggests that beginning museum visitors can feel uncomfortable forming or sharing their own opinions (Shaer et al., 2011).

Borrowed Eyes provides a simple starting point through visual details and clues. Visitors do not need specialist art knowledge. They can begin with observation and personal response.

Visitors are also **contributors**. Their clues and interpretations become part of the experience for future visitors.

### 2.2 Secondary Stakeholders — Gallery and Museum Staff

Relevant gallery staff include:

- gallery educators / learning staff;
- visitor experience staff;
- gallery guides / volunteers;
- curatorial / interpretation staff;
- digital experience staff.

They may not directly participate in Borrowed Eyes, but they are important if the concept is used in a real gallery.

Their main concerns may include visitor engagement, accessibility, moderation, visitor-generated content, artwork selection, and technical limits.

### 2.3 Secondary Stakeholders — Non-Participating Visitors

Non-participating visitors also share the physical gallery space.

Borrowed Eyes participants may move between artworks, search for details, and spend more time around particular works.

We do not currently have evidence that this creates disruption. It is therefore a **design consideration**, not a confirmed problem.

Future testing can consider movement, accessibility, crowding, and the viewing experience of other visitors.

---

## 3. Primary Stakeholder Needs

Based on our research and current design direction, we identified five main needs.

| Need | Description |
|---|---|
| **Easy starting point** | Visitors need a simple way to begin looking at unfamiliar artworks. |
| **Interpretation confidence** | Visitors should be able to respond without specialist art knowledge or a “correct answer.” |
| **Low-pressure social connection** | Visitors should be able to see other perspectives without speaking directly to strangers. |
| **Physical artwork attention** | The activity should encourage visitors to look closely at physical artworks. |
| **Simple contribution** | Visitors should be able to leave a short clue or interpretation for another visitor. |

---

## 4. Stakeholder Engagement Plan

Different stakeholders can provide different types of information.

| Stakeholder | How We Can Reach Them | What We Want to Learn |
|---|---|---|
| **Casual / less-experienced visitors** | Short interviews, gallery observation, and prototype testing | Interpretation confidence, observation, response to other perspectives, and willingness to contribute |
| **Gallery educators / learning staff** | Contact the gallery learning team by email | Common interpretation difficulties and ways to support visitor learning |
| **Visitor experience staff** | Gallery contact or staff during gallery visits | Visitor behaviour, movement, and practical visitor needs |
| **Gallery guides / volunteers** | Speak with guides or contact the volunteer team | Common visitor questions and what encourages closer observation |
| **Curatorial / interpretation staff** | Contact by email | How visitor-generated content could work with gallery information |
| **Digital experience staff** | Contact digital or experience design staff | Accessibility, technical limits, and digital distraction |
| **Non-participating visitors** | Observation during later testing | Possible effects on movement, access, and normal viewing |

---

## 5. Stakeholder Relationship

Borrowed Eyes creates an indirect relationship between visitors across time.

> **Gallery / Museum Staff**  
> support the experience  
> ↓  
> **Visitor A**  
> leaves a detail, clue, and perspective  
> ↓  
> **Visitor B**  
> explores the artwork and forms their own interpretation  
> ↓  
> compares perspectives  
> ↓  
> **Visitor B becomes a contributor**  
> ↓  
> **Future Visitor**

This relationship shows the main change in our stakeholder understanding:

> **Visitor = User + Contributor**

Visitors do not only receive an experience. They also help shape the experience of future visitors.

---

## 6. Stakeholder Needs and Design Implications

| Stakeholder Need / Consideration | Design Implication |
|---|---|
| Visitors may need an easy starting point | Use a visual fragment and short clue |
| Visitors may lack interpretation confidence | Focus on personal response instead of specialist knowledge |
| Other perspectives may support reflection | Show another visitor's interpretation after the participant forms their own |
| Direct stranger interaction may feel uncomfortable | Keep social interaction anonymous and low-pressure |
| Visitors are also contributors | Allow participants to create content for future visitors |
| Physical artworks should remain central | Use searching and observation as part of the interaction |
| Visitor-generated content may need management | Consider moderation and content management |
| The gallery is a shared space | Consider accessibility, movement, and non-participating visitors |

---

## 7. Conclusion

The **primary stakeholders** are casual and less-experienced gallery visitors. Their needs and experiences should have the strongest influence on our design.

Gallery staff and non-participating visitors are secondary stakeholders. They help us understand the practical, social, and physical context of the experience.

The development from Art Persona to Borrowed Eyes also changed our understanding of the visitor:

> **Visitor = User + Contributor**

Our current prototype focuses on whether visitors can observe artworks more closely, form their own interpretations, compare different perspectives, and contribute something for a future visitor.

Later research should also consider moderation, accessibility, content management, and gallery operations.
