# Design Iteration 3 — Borrowed Eyes: Three Participation Modes

## 1. Iteration Overview

Borrowed Eyes explores artworks as **shared conversation anchors**. The aim is to create opportunities for visitors to express personal responses, encounter different perspectives, and develop mutual understanding, rather than treating correct artwork identification as the only outcome.

This iteration develops the earlier **Card–Clue–Comment** concept into three participation options: **Solo**, **Blind Eyes**, and **Face-to-Face**. The earlier design explored how one visitor's observation could shape another visitor's experience asynchronously. The newer concepts explore different levels of direct social interaction, while retaining artwork-centred exploration.

> **Design opportunity:** There is an opportunity to use artworks as shared conversation anchors, helping gallery visitors move beyond discussing art to sharing personal experiences, understanding different backgrounds, and building meaningful human connections.

## 2. Evidence Informing the Iteration

Our exploratory survey included **30 respondents**. Selected results informed our consideration of visitors' preferences and barriers:

| Evidence | Design consideration |
| --- | --- |
| 21/30 (70%) selected quiet artwork viewing as something they enjoy. | Preserve an independent participation option. |
| 9/30 (30%) reported confidence sharing interpretations with other visitors. | Avoid making direct social interaction compulsory. |
| 10/30 (33%) agreed anonymity would increase comfort. | Do not assume anonymity alone addresses social barriers. |
| 13/30 (43%) were concerned that a Blind Eyes partner might not respond. | Clarify communication expectations and consider recovery routes. |
| 14/30 (47%) were concerned about a person dominating group discussion. | Explore how each participant can contribute. |
| 22/30 (73%) wanted to know the activity duration before joining. | Communicate expectations before participation. |

These are descriptive findings from a small, exploratory sample; they do not represent all gallery visitors.

On **14 October 2026**, we conducted **four Blind Eyes rounds** and **one Face-to-Face group session**. The same people appeared across some rounds and modes, so these are not independent samples. Blind Eyes recorded exchanges of interpretations, but some participants used subjective questions mainly to eliminate candidate artworks. Face-to-Face interviews described shared clues, discussion and collective decisions; observation counts and confirmed task success were not recorded for that mode.

For full evidence, see the repository's survey findings, survey results and latest prototype testing report. This page presents **design reasoning**, rather than replacing those reports.

## 3. Design Concepts

### 3.1 Solo — Independent Engagement

**Intent:** Offer a quieter route for visitors who prefer to explore and reflect on artworks independently.

**Proposed experience:** A visitor chooses an individual activity, explores physical artworks and records or considers their own interpretation without needing to speak to another visitor.

**Design value:** Flexible, lower-pressure participation. Solo does **not** promise direct interpersonal connection; it acknowledges different social preferences.

**Evidence status:** Informed by survey preferences. The **14 October testing did not evaluate Solo**. Any digital implementation should be described separately from the tested paper concepts.

### 3.2 Blind Eyes — Two-Person Perspective Exchange

**Intent:** Use unequal access to information to create a reason to ask questions, explain observations and compare interpretations.

**Tested mechanism:** Two participants take the roles of **Eye** and **Finder**. The Eye knows the target artwork; the Finder asks questions to identify it. The Finder can ask **up to three factual questions** and **unlimited perspective questions**. The roles can switch in later rounds.

**Social opportunity:** Visitors may talk about mood, meaning, details and personal associations; after the reveal, both can compare how they understood the same work.

**Observed tension:** Conversation can remain task-focused, and the Eye may avoid volunteering more interpretation for fear of giving away the answer. The question limit was **not compared against an unlimited-condition test**, so its causal effect is unknown.

**Next design hypothesis:** A short, optional **post-reveal comparison** may support more reciprocal explanation. This step is proposed for a future iteration, not an already validated feature.

### 3.3 Face-to-Face — Small-Group Collaborative Exploration

**Intent:** Investigate **communication, coordination and cooperation** through an artwork-finding task.

**Tested mechanism:** Three participants each receive different perspective clues and search the gallery together **without predefined roles**. Participants share clues, compare candidate artworks and attempt a collective decision.

- **Communication:** Share observations, feelings, interpretations and disagreements.
- **Coordination:** Decide where to search, how to compare clues and whether to divide responsibilities.
- **Cooperation:** Use different members' contributions in a shared decision, rather than letting one person complete the activity alone.

**Observed tension:** Interview accounts differed on whether the team divided the search into areas. A participant found it harder to speak up. The group described making a collective choice, but the recorded outcome did **not confirm whether the answer was correct**.

**Next design hypothesis:** Distributing distinctive clues, checking that contributions are heard, and observing decisions directly may help evaluate balanced collaboration. These are priorities for further testing, not confirmed effects.

## 4. Relationship Between the Modes

| Mode | Participation level | Main interaction | Current validation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solo | Individual | Independent exploration and reflection | Survey-informed; not tested in the latest sessions |
| Blind Eyes | Paired | Question-led exchange under information asymmetry | Four formative rounds |
| Face-to-Face | Small group | Clue sharing, negotiation and collective choice | One three-person formative session |

The three modes are **alternatives**, not a required progression. Visitors should not have to complete Solo before joining a social mode.

## 5. Design Principles and Trade-offs

1. **Artwork first:** Digital or paper prompts should draw attention back to physical artworks.
2. **Flexible social participation:** Offer different degrees of interaction, with clear expectations and an exit route.
3. **Perspective before performance:** Measure explanation, listening and reconsideration separately from finding the right artwork.
4. **Meaningful contributions:** In group work, investigate whether members' observations actually affect the shared decision.
5. **Do not force disclosure:** Sharing personal experiences must remain voluntary.
6. **Be clear about prototype status:** A tested paper interaction is not automatically a completed digital feature.

## 6. Evaluation Questions for the Next Iteration

- When do participants exchange interpretations rather than only clues?
- Can each person explain why another visitor sees an artwork differently?
- Do participants voluntarily relate artworks to personal feelings or experiences?
- How are plans proposed, accepted, changed and enacted during group work?
- Whose contributions shape the final choice, and whose may be overlooked?
- Do visitors understand duration, partner/group expectations and ways to opt out?

## 7. Current Status and Next Steps

**Established:** Exploratory survey; four Blind Eyes testing rounds; one Face-to-Face group test; evidence of information exchange and some negotiation.

**Not established:** Comparative effectiveness of modes; causal effect of anonymity or factual-question limits; equal participation; sustained mutual understanding; meaningful human connections; correct Face-to-Face task completion.

**Proposed next steps:** Refine joining instructions, clarify Eye/Finder rules, test a post-reveal reflection stage, record group coordination and contributions, and assess social comfort. Update the digital implementation status when features are actually built and tested.
