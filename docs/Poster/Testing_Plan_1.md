# Borrowed Eyes Testing Plan

## 1. Test Overview

This formative test evaluates the **Borrowed Eyes low-fidelity paper prototype** in a physical art gallery.

The test focuses on the visitor experience rather than visual interface design.

The main purpose is to understand whether Borrowed Eyes can:

- create curiosity through visual fragments;
- encourage closer observation of physical artworks;
- support personal interpretation without specialist art knowledge;
- introduce another visitor's perspective without replacing the participant's own view;
- create low-pressure social connection; and
- encourage visitors to contribute content for future visitors.

The test also examines whether the full interaction can work as a cumulative visitor-to-visitor experience.

The core interaction is:

> **Choose → Find → Interpret → Compare → Re-observe → Contribute**

---

## 2. Design Assumptions

Before testing, we identified six assumptions:

| ID | Design Assumption |
|---|---|
| **A1** | Visual fragments and progressive clues can create curiosity and support artwork search. |
| **A2** | Visitors can form a personal interpretation without specialist art knowledge. |
| **A3** | Visitors should form their own interpretation before seeing another visitor's perspective. |
| **A4** | Another visitor's perspective can encourage reflection, re-observation, or a different way of looking. |
| **A5** | Different perspectives can be useful without agreement, supporting low-pressure social interaction. |
| **A6** | Visitors can move from receiving another person's contribution to creating content for a future visitor. |

---

## 3. Research Questions

### Primary Research Question

**Can Borrowed Eyes use visitor-generated visual fragments, clues, and interpretations to support closer observation and low-pressure social interpretation in a physical gallery?**

### Secondary Research Questions

1. Can visual fragments and clues support artwork search and closer observation?
2. Can visitors form their own interpretation without specialist art knowledge?
3. Does another visitor's perspective encourage reflection or re-observation?
4. Can different perspectives be useful without requiring agreement?
5. Can visitors successfully create content for a future visitor?

---

## 4. Participants and Recruitment

### Target Participants

The main target participants are **casual and less-experienced gallery visitors**.

Borrowed Eyes is designed for visitors who may have limited art knowledge or may feel unsure about forming their own interpretation.

This is a **formative qualitative test**. The purpose is to identify behaviours, usability problems, and design opportunities rather than produce statistically representative results.

### Planned Recruitment

The testing process includes two participant groups:

- **4 initial contributors** who create the starting visitor-generated content;
- **6 testing participants** who complete the Borrowed Eyes testing flow.

The 4 initial contributors provide the content needed to begin the visitor-to-visitor interaction.

The 6 testing participants are the main participants used to evaluate the Borrowed Eyes experience.

### Team Organisation

The team has **5 members**.

**Team Member 1** will recruit 4 initial contributors and prepare the four starting cards.

**Team Members 2–5** will recruit a total of 6 testing participants and facilitate the main testing sessions.

---

## 5. Prototype Materials

Prepare the following materials:

| Material | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Fragment Cards** | Show cropped visual details from selected artworks |
| **Clue 1** | Gives a short clue without revealing the answer |
| **Optional Clue 2** | Provides extra help if the participant is stuck |
| **Participant Response Sheet** | Records independent interpretation and comparison |
| **Previous Visitor Comment** | Revealed only after the participant forms their own interpretation |
| **Leave Your Eyes Sheet** | Allows the participant to create content for the next visitor |
| **Phone / Camera** | Records the visual detail selected by the participant |
| **Facilitator Notes** | Records behaviour, help needed, and important comments |

The previous visitor's interpretation must remain hidden until the participant completes their own response.

---

## 6. Testing Procedure

### Stage 1 — Choose

Show the participant several fragment cards.

Ask:

> **Which visual fragment makes you most curious?**

The participant chooses one card.

**Observe:**

- Which card is selected?
- Why does the fragment attract attention?
- Does the participant understand that they need to find the complete artwork?

Do not explain where the artwork is located.

---

### Stage 2 — Find

Give the participant **Clue 1**.

Ask them to move through the gallery and find the artwork.

If they cannot find it after trying, provide **Clue 2**.

**Observe:**

- Can the participant find the artwork?
- Do they need Clue 2?
- How do they search?
- Do they look more closely at artworks?
- Is the clue too easy or too difficult?

The facilitator should not point directly to the correct artwork.

---

### Stage 3 — Interpret Independently

After the participant finds the artwork, do **not** reveal the previous visitor's comment.

Ask:

> **What did you notice first when you saw the complete artwork?**

Then ask:

> **What does the selected detail make you think or feel?**

The participant records their response.

**Observe:**

- Can they respond without specialist art knowledge?
- Do they describe visual details?
- Do they express feelings or personal interpretations?
- Do they appear unsure about giving an answer?

There is no correct interpretation.

---

### Stage 4 — Reveal and Compare

After the independent response, reveal the previous visitor's comment.

Ask whether the participant:
- agrees, partly agrees, disagrees, or noticed something different;
- noticed something new, reconsidered their interpretation, or kept the same view.

Ask:

> **Did the other visitor's perspective change how you looked at the artwork? How?**

Observe whether the participant reflects further or returns attention to the artwork.
---

### Stage 5 — Re-observe

Give the participant time to look at the artwork again after seeing the previous interpretation.

**Observe:**

- Do they return their attention to the artwork?
- Do they inspect new areas or details?
- Do they spend additional time looking?
- Do they explain any change in their interpretation?

This stage helps test whether the social information redirects attention back to the physical artwork.

---

### Stage 6 — Contribute: Leave Your Eyes

Ask the participant to choose a different artwork.

They then:

1. select one visual detail;
2. photograph or identify the detail;
3. create a short clue;
4. write a short personal interpretation.

Explain that this material may be used by a future participant.

**Observe:**

- Can they select a meaningful visual detail?
- Can they create a clue without revealing the answer?
- Can they write a personal interpretation?
- Do they understand that another visitor will use their contribution?
- Are they willing to contribute?

This stage tests the transition:

> **Visitor → Contributor**

---

### Post-Test Questions

After the activity, ask:

1. How did you feel about searching with a visual detail and clue?
2. Was the clue too easy, too difficult, or about right?
3. Did the activity make you look more closely at the artwork?
4. How did you feel about seeing another visitor's interpretation?
5. Did their perspective make you notice or think about anything new?
6. How did you feel about creating something for the next visitor?

Use neutral follow-up questions when needed:

> **Why?**

> **Can you tell me more about that?**

> **What made you think that?**

## 7. Testing Structure

The testing process has two parts.

### Part 1 — Prepare Initial Visitor Content

**Team Member 1 + 4 initial contributors**

Each initial contributor will help create one starting card by:

1. choosing an artwork;
2. selecting one visual detail;
3. creating a cropped fragment;
4. writing a short clue;
5. leaving a personal interpretation.

The output will be:

> **4 visitor-created Fragment + Clue + Interpretation cards**

These cards provide the starting content for the main testing.

### Part 2 — Test the Borrowed Eyes Experience

**Team Members 2–5 + 6 testing participants**

Each testing participant completes:

> **Choose → Find → Interpret → Compare → Re-observe → Contribute**

The content created by participants can be prepared for later participants.

This begins the cumulative social structure:

> **Previous visitor leaves a trace**  
> ↓  
> **Current visitor follows it**  
> ↓  
> **Current visitor interprets and compares**  
> ↓  
> **Current visitor leaves a new trace**  
> ↓  
> **Future visitor**

The intended interaction can therefore be summarised as:

> **Borrow → Interpret → Leave**

---

## 8. Data Collection and Evidence Recording

### Data Collection

For each of the 6 testing participants, the team plans to collect:

- selected card;
- whether the artwork was found without help;
- whether Clue 2 was needed;
- independent interpretation;
- response to the previous visitor's interpretation;
- whether something new was noticed;
- whether the interpretation changed or stayed the same;
- participant-created visual detail;
- participant-created clue;
- participant-created interpretation;
- facilitator observation notes;
- testing photographs where appropriate.

This evidence will later be used to compare the design assumptions with participant behaviour.

### Evidence Recording

Three types of evidence should be recorded separately.

#### Participant Statement

What the participant actually says or writes.

Use a direct quote only when the exact wording is recorded.

#### Observation

What the facilitator directly observes.

Example:

> Participant looked back at the artwork after reading the previous visitor's comment.

#### Researcher Interpretation

What the team thinks the behaviour may mean.

Example:

> The previous visitor's perspective may have encouraged re-observation.

Participant statements, observations, and researcher interpretations should not be mixed.

If exact wording is not recorded, use **facilitator paraphrase** rather than presenting it as a direct quote.

---

## 9. Evaluation Framework

After testing, each design assumption will be reviewed using the collected evidence.

| Assumption | Evidence to Look For |
|---|---|
| **A1 — Search and observation** | Participant uses the fragment and clues to explore and locate the artwork |
| **A2 — Personal interpretation** | Participant forms a personal response without specialist art knowledge |
| **A3 — Independent response** | Participant forms their own response before seeing another perspective |
| **A4 — Reflection and re-observation** | Participant looks again, notices something new, or reflects further |
| **A5 — Perspective difference** | Participant can value another perspective without needing to agree |
| **A6 — Contribution** | Participant creates a fragment, clue, and interpretation for a future visitor |

After the test, each assumption can be classified as:

- **Supported**
- **Partially Supported**
- **Needs Refinement**
- **Not Yet Tested**

These categories are used to guide design decisions rather than provide statistical proof.

### Facilitator Guidelines

The facilitator should:

- explain that there is no correct interpretation;
- remain neutral;
- allow participants to search independently;
- reveal Clue 2 only when needed;
- keep previous comments hidden until the independent response is complete;
- record participant behaviour and comments;
- avoid suggesting interpretations or encouraging agreement.

---

## 10. Testing Scope

This round focuses on the core Borrowed Eyes experience:

- artwork search and closer observation;
- clue usability;
- independent interpretation;
- comparison with another visitor's perspective;
- re-observation;
- visitor contribution;
- the cumulative visitor-to-visitor interaction.

As this is a **paper prototype**, this round does not evaluate:

- final digital interface design;
- mobile screen usability or distraction;
- location-aware technology;
- digital notifications;
- long-term content moderation;
- large-scale gallery deployment.

These areas can be explored in later prototype iterations.

---

## Expected Outcome

This test is not designed to prove that Borrowed Eyes improves every gallery experience.

Instead, it will help the team identify:

- which design assumptions are supported by participant behaviour;
- which assumptions need further testing;
- which interaction problems need refinement;
- what should change in the next prototype.

The testing results will later be analysed using the following structure:

> **Design Assumption → Testing Evidence → Finding → Design Problem → Next Iteration**
